How to Tell If You Have Worms

The most common signs of pinworms and other intestinal parasites, what causes them, and when at-home treatment is enough.

Intestinal worms in humans are more common than most people realize — pinworms in particular spread easily in households, daycares, and schools, and having them is not a sign of poor hygiene. Most cases are mild and treatable with an over-the-counter medication. Here's what to watch for.

1. Itching around the anus, especially at night

This is the single most recognizable sign of pinworms. Female pinworms lay eggs around the anus at night, which causes intense itching that's often worse after going to bed. It's common in kids but happens in adults too, especially within the same household.

2. Visible worms

Pinworms are small, thin, and white — often described as looking like small pieces of thread, roughly a quarter to half an inch long. They're sometimes visible in stool or, less pleasantly, around the anal area at night with a flashlight a few hours after falling asleep, which is actually a standard way to confirm a pinworm infection at home.

3. Restless sleep or trouble sleeping

Because pinworm activity and itching peak at night, disrupted sleep — especially in kids who seem unusually restless or irritable the next day — is a common secondary sign.

4. Stomach discomfort or nausea

Mild abdominal pain, cramping, or occasional nausea can occur, though these symptoms overlap with a lot of other common issues, so they're rarely the first clue on their own.

5. Irritability or difficulty concentrating (in kids)

Combined with the sleep disruption above, kids with pinworms sometimes seem more irritable or have a harder time focusing during the day — often mistaken for something else until the nighttime itching is noticed.

6. Weight loss or reduced appetite (less common)

More typical of other types of intestinal parasites than pinworms specifically, unexplained weight loss alongside digestive symptoms is worth mentioning to a doctor, particularly after travel to an area with different sanitation standards.

7. Someone else in your household has been diagnosed

Pinworms spread very easily between people who share a home, especially among children. If one family member is diagnosed, it's common (and often recommended) to treat the whole household at the same time, since eggs can survive on shared surfaces, bedding, and clothing for up to two weeks.

When to see a doctor instead of treating at home Children under 2, anyone who is pregnant, and anyone with symptoms that persist after a first round of OTC treatment should check with a doctor rather than repeating treatment on their own. Severe abdominal pain, significant blood in stool, or symptoms following international travel are also reasons to get a proper diagnosis rather than guessing.
